1-Page Take-Home Cheat Sheet

Making slides and presentations with AI

Ruidong · JazLab, MIT · One source → minimal stage deck + standalone handout

Write the note once.
AI makes sure you follow it.
1 · Check your own rules

Write 3 rules you believe in (or ask an AI to infer them from your past slides). Run a checker that never gets tired at 2 a.m.

10-min test: Paste your 3 rules + a draft deck into Claude and ask it to flag violations without rewriting your voice.
2 · Own your figure pipeline

Keep the script and data linked to the figure so edits reach the plotting code, not a screenshot or menu of features.

10-min test: Pick one key figure in your next talk and link its code so changing a color or threshold rebuilds cleanly.
3 · A second pair of eyes

Record yourself rehearsing aloud. Give the AI only the transcript—no slides, no notes—so it hears what the room hears.

10-min test: Ask the agent: “Summarize what my talk was about and where I assumed jargon.” If it misses your point, revise.

Keep · Rules about human attention (Winston)

  • Open with a clear promise & end on contributions
  • Say your core idea three times in different ways
  • Keep the visual surface uncluttered (one takeaway at a time)
  • Human owns taste and final decisions

Rethink · Habits caused by production cost

  • 2×3 static subplot grids → one live plot with sliders
  • 30 hidden backup slides → clickable drill-down toggles right on the figure
  • Static schematics → quick code-driven animations (Manimble / HTML)
  • Deck-as-handout compromise → one source, separate stage deck + cheat sheet
Tools mentioned: Claude + Google Slides · Livedeck · Manimble (Pachter lab) · Interactive HTML decks
